# Game structure

The browser entry handles one observed startup chain. `GameSession` owns the active scene and frame order, while input, movement, camera, rendering, character content, and the world are separate modules. The session plays one character at a time from the roster and one desert world.

## Characters

`src/content/transformer/` is the shared transformer runtime every character is built on: asset decoding (`asset/`), track playback and the live rig (`model/`), the procedural gait (`animation/`), transformation and footfall audio (`audio/`), cue merging (`cues.ts`) and the `Character` contract (`character.ts`). Geometry and choreography are authored in each character's Blender build, not in TypeScript.

Each character package (`src/content/cybertruck/`, `src/content/ferrari-f1/`, `src/content/semi/`, `src/content/bat/`) supplies only what is its own: materials for its slot names, its effects and sounds, its gait style, its fighting (`combat/`: four moves, its weapon and effects, combat.md; its special and the special's effects, specials.md) and a `CharacterProfile` (drive limits, robot speeds, camera framing, collision radii). A character's weapon is a separate asset loaded with it (weapons.md). The game modules (`game/movement.ts`, `game/follow-camera.ts`) take these values from the profile. Tuning a car means editing its profile, not the game code.

The container has optional parts a character may use (semi.md, bat.md): meshes with a second, tailored shape blended by a per-frame weight; world-hosted nodes `carried` by a bone once the skeleton is live; a `trailer` node swinging about its hitch by the car's articulation; front tyres of their own radius (`frontWheelRadius`). Its profile may add a trailer (car-handling.md), a long car's collision circles (`carBody`) and a car framing centred off its origin (`carAhead`, `carHalfLength`).

`src/content/roster.ts` lists the playable cars: label, menu tagline and accent, loader and factory. Adding a car means adding a package and a roster entry. Assets download once per session (the promise is shared; a failed download can be retried). The last choice is remembered in local storage and used at boot.

## Enemies and the fortress

The desert's fortress (`src/worlds/desert/fort/`, forts.md) is world scenery; its districts' garrisons are `src/game/enemies/` (enemies.md), with the soldier's asset, rig, rendering and health bars in `src/content/soldier/` (soldier-model.md). The session owns one `Horde` (every garrison) and one `CarBarrier`. It routes the fight's `onHit` events (and its vacuums, `onPull`) to the horde and the horde's blade strikes to the playing character's `struck`, tells the horde while a special plays (`Horde.special`: nothing is destroyed before its last blow) and settles the horde as the cutscene ends. The soldier asset loads at boot beside the car's, as its own observed stage.

## Switching cars

`GameSession.switchCharacter` swaps cars in either form, fighting or not, inside a fortress or out: a combo or a raised guard is simply dropped. Asked during a transformation, a jump or a special it waits for it to end (the world runs on meanwhile; the freeze starts only once it is swapping) instead of refusing: refusing made the menu look broken until the right moment was hit. The selection is locked while a car loads. A car is built the first time it is chosen:
- download its vehicle and weapon assets together; the roster load promise is typed as `PlayableTransformerAsset`, so it cannot report ready without the weapon;
- place it where the current car stands;
- compile its shaders against the live scene's lighting (`compileAsync(object, camera, scene)`), then keep the combat weapon/effects warm through the hidden settle frames so geometry uploads plus the weapon cast-shadow pipeline are submitted before the cover lifts.

Built cars stay cached (GPU resources warm) for switching back. On the swap:
- the old car's voices stop;
- a standing robot keeps its standing point (the car origin shifts by the difference in robot stations);
- one contact-free surface update ends the old tyre ribbons.

A cached car switches synchronously when the player is free to switch: it restores silent audio voices and the menu does not show its loading cover, mute the mix, wait for settle frames, or drain the GPU queue again. If a transformation, jump, or special is still playing, the request waits for that action first. First-use cars keep the covered GPU warmup and settle frames.

The in-game UI is the vehicle menu (`src/ui/vehicle-menu.ts`), the special's energy meter in the top-left corner and the cutscene's letterbox bars (`src/ui/energy-meter.ts`, specials.md) and the pause menu (see Pausing). The menu cannot open during a cutscene, and the meter and pill hide under it. Collapsed, it is a small hint pill at the bottom of the screen (`Tab` to switch, `R` to transform; while the pointer is released it says to click to play). Opening the menu grows the same glass panel out of the pill into a carousel of car names. The panel is laid out at full size the whole time and one `clip-path` transition does the morph, so nothing reflows. The pill's width comes from the measured label (`--hint-w`) and is measured again only on resize or a label change. Tab opens the menu without releasing the pointer lock: mouse look and game keys pause while it is open, and the keyboard drives it. The open menu also holds the game still, exactly as the pause menu does but with no pause UI (see Pausing). Escape still releases the pointer (browsers reserve it), which leaves the menu open for the mouse. The arrow keys, the arrow buttons (once the pointer is free), a horizontal mouse drag or trackpad scroll, or a click on a faded neighbour only move the selection; nothing loads until it is confirmed with Enter (or a click on the centred name), and the status line says so. Confirming the car already driven just closes the menu. While a first-use car loads, a dark cover with a loading rule hides the screen. The controls lock (like a transformation), the menu ignores closing, and the audio output is held silent (`AudioMix.hold`); the voices keep running. The swap happens behind the cover. That first-use switch resolves only after a few more frames are drawn and the GPU queue drains, so uploads and pipeline builds land out of sight. The cover lifts, the sound fades in and the menu closes back to play, all at once. A failed load leaves the menu open with the reason. Before this, the new engine could be heard while the picture was still stalled on those first frames. Tab, Escape or the backdrop closes the menu without switching and resumes play.

## Pausing

The pause menu (`src/ui/pause-menu.ts`) is a dimmed, blurred cover over the frozen frame with a Resume plate. Browsers keep Escape for releasing pointer lock and may never deliver the key, so pause is driven by lock loss during play. That covers Escape, the window losing focus and a tab switch. The exception is while the vehicle menu is open, because the vehicle menu owns the pointer and Escape then. When that menu closes without getting the lock back (it was closed by Escape, or closed itself after a load with no gesture), `PauseMenu.settle()` pauses the game. Otherwise play would carry on with a free pointer and no overlay. Resuming has to take the lock again, and Escape does not count as a user gesture. A click, Enter or Space resumes. The game is released on the lock-change event, not on the button press. A refused lock leaves the menu up and shakes the button. Chrome refuses a lock requested right after the player released one. `FollowCamera.activate()` resolves from the lock events for this, since not every browser returns a promise from `requestPointerLock`. The vehicle menu cannot open while the game is paused.

The game is held through `GameSession.hold(reason, held)`, and it runs again once no reason is left. There are two reasons. `'pause'` is the pause menu. `'menu'` is the vehicle menu while it is open and not switching. A switch releases the `'menu'` hold for as long as it runs, because it may have to wait out a transformation, jump or special (the world has to run for that to end). A first-use car also has to draw its settle frames under the cover. If the switch fails and the menu stays open, the hold comes back. While held, `GameSession.frame()` does no work, so nothing is simulated or drawn. The animation loop keeps running as a no-op instead of being cleared, so `GpuHost.fail` stays the only thing that stops it. A resize clears the canvas, so the frozen scene is drawn once more whenever the drawing buffer changes size. On resume the timer is reset, so no time passes for the world. `AudioMix.pause` fades the output over a few milliseconds and then suspends the context, which freezes every voice and scheduled event where it stood. Interaction-driven `resume()` does not wake the context while the game is paused. The pause adds no render paths (it is DOM only), so the warm-up does not need to cover it.

## Audio

`src/audio/mix.ts` owns the one AudioContext, the bus compressor, air lowpass, the outdoor reverb and the pre-rendered textures. Entry loading creates the context and textures, prepares the starting car's silent continuous voices, and renders the soldier strike bank. Playback still resumes on the first user interaction. A first-use car prepares its silent voices while its switch cover is up. `dispose()` stops continuous voices when the car leaves the scene; cached cars rebuild those voices when they play again.

## World and motion

Baked noise and the shared image setup (`rendering/look.ts`: tone mapping, shadows, environment bake, bloom) live outside the character packages. The session and the headless preview tool use the same setup.

`src/worlds/desert/` owns scenery, lighting, ground materials, dust, tyre tracks, and collision circles. The ground's relief reaches the game through `Ground` (`game/ground.ts`: a height at any world point, plus a normal and a ray march built on it). The world's `DesertTerrain` is one, and `ContactEffects.height` exposes the same to effects. Car dynamics, the robot's footing (`game/ground-follow.ts`), the follow camera and the combat effects' ground contacts all ask it; nothing assumes y = 0. Its entry point supplies the world, environment lighting scene, and contact effects. Characters receive contact effects through a small interface, so another environment can provide different tyre and footfall feedback without changing character code.

The repeating rock field checks its tile positions as the camera moves, but only recomposes and uploads an instance when that rock actually wraps into a different tile. Its positions and collision footprints remain the same.

Gameplay motion uses an explicit state for position, heading, forward and lateral velocity, tyre slide, form target, suspension, and transform progress. Car handling is `game/car-dynamics.ts` (car-handling.md); robot gait and jumps are robot-locomotion.md. The two locomotion paths share that state. Combat (combat.md) is a system around the session: `RobotCombat` owns the combo and the special, writes the fight's root motion into the same state and lays its pose over the gait through the model's rig overlay. The special's director and the camera reactions and hit-stop (`CameraFx`) sit after the follow camera, in that order, and the special's tempo scales the world's clock (specials.md). Future racing and flight should be added the same way, rather than extending the loop with mode-specific branches.

All source modules under `src/` are TypeScript. The asset tests cover pose and handover invariants alongside the compiler checks; the Blender audits cover geometry and mechanism clearance.
