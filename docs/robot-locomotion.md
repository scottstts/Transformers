# Robot locomotion

The procedural gait (`src/content/transformer/animation/gait.ts`) and jump timeline (`src/game/jump.ts`) are shared by every robot; each character supplies a `GaitStyle` (step, lift, sway, hip and shoulder motion, heel/toe roll angles, and its sole: heel and toe edges from the ankle, ankle height). The rig (`model/rig.ts`) turns the channels into the pelvis frame, torso and head rotations and the two-bone leg IK.

## Walking and running

Shift switches walking to running. Walking contact occupies 58 % of the cycle; running contact occupies 36 % for the truck, 26 % for the Semi and 20 % for the F1, leaving a flight interval between steps. Step lengths (one full cycle covers two steps) are 2.45 / 4.6 m walking / running for the truck, 1.95 / 5.2 m for the F1, 3.3 / 5.6 m for the Semi and 2.18 / 4.1 m for the Bat, at 3.9 / 15, 3.2 / 15.6, 4.5 / 17 and 3.7 / 14.2 m/s. Every walk sits at Froude 0.5, so a bigger body walks faster at a slower cadence. Tune speed together with stride length: the pair sets the cadence (see Timing and scale).

## Timing and scale

A leg swings under gravity, so a body's natural step rate falls with the square root of its leg length. Stepping faster than that reads as sped-up film, a comic walk, whatever the joint poses. `node tools/gait-probe.mjs` reports each gait against a human scaled to the robot's hip height (dynamic similarity):

- **Froude number** v² / (g · hip height). Walking is only possible below about 1, and people break into a run near 0.5. The F1 walked at 1.13 (4.8 m/s on 2.08 m hips), past the physical limit of a walk, and the truck and Semi at 0.85. All three now walk at 0.5.
- **Dimensionless cadence**, steps per second × √(hip height / g). A brisk human walk is about 0.65–0.75, and a sprint about 1.3–1.4. The F1 walks at 0.75 and sprints at 1.37. The truck and Semi walk at 0.89, with slightly shorter steps (0.79 hip heights) on their shorter soles. Their runs still step at 1.8–2.0 (about 1.4 times as quick as human-similar); the runs read as heavy machines and were left as they are.
- **Vertical acceleration** of the rendered pelvis, sampled at 60 Hz. The walk's rise and fall and the run's bounce should stay within a few g. A walk that vaults 10 cm at 3 steps/s accelerates at about 2 g, a hop, and a one-frame kink shows as tens of g.

Lengths scale with the robot and times with its square root. Tuning a stride for its look while keeping a step rate that was set for a smaller body cannot be fixed with joint angles, and several F1 passes tried.

- **Ground contact.** At a settled walk or run, a stance foot sweeps back exactly what the body travels while it is down (`2 × stance share × step length`). At starts, stops and pivots, the sweep fades with amplitude and a damped translation share. The cadence floor never imposes a minimum foot excursion, which would snap the feet outward at startup. Below that floor, settling the feet takes priority over exact contact speed.
- **Heel to toe.** The foot lands toe-up on the heel, rolls flat over the first 20 % of stance and peels onto the toe over the last 40 %, pivoting about the sole's measured heel and toe edges. The edges must match the mesh (`--sole` in the probe). A rounded edge (`soleRadius`) rolls instead of pivoting: `heel` and `toe` are then the roundings' centres, and the ankle rides an arc about a centre that advances r·φ along the ground (`animation/sole.ts`, shared by the stride, the jump's toe push and the fight's heel raise). A sole whose bottom rakes in the stand is levelled while stepping by `soleTilt` (the stand is unchanged), and `ankle` is then the height of the levelled edges. A sole whose flat bottom bulges below its edge line bears on the bulge until an edge takes over; `belly` (its forward place and depth) lets the ankle stand on whichever reaches lowest, so the switch does not drop the body within a frame or two (12 mm on the Semi). The measured values (F1 heel 0.17 / toe 0.75 with 5 cm rounding and a flat, unraked sole; truck 0.29 / 0.62 / 4 mm belly; Semi 0.42 / 1.02 / 12 mm belly; Bat 0.33 / 0.86) fit the mesh within 1–3 mm through the roll (the F1 within 1 mm; the Bat's chamfered toe within 1 cm by the toe-off). The ground projection must see the same edges: its 26 box-direction support points meet a rounding only at 45°, so on the F1 it found the heel 4–6 mm higher than the mesh through the 5–30° heel roll and dropped the body at every foot-flat (5 g). The F1 and the Bat also sample their soles along their rolling plane (`rollSupport`, pitch −60..60° measured in the stand): the box directions found the Bat's heel up to 7 cm high and its walk jolted at 6 g at every foot-flat (about 3 g with it). The truck and Semi keep box directions, which their sole values were fitted against. The Semi's toe had been modelled 7 cm short, driving its sole 2–4 cm into the ground at every toe-off. The swing matches the full stance velocity at both ends, but those tangent terms fade quickly (`SWING_TANGENT_FADE`): a cubic Hermite carried them through the whole swing and, with the short run stance, flung the feet 0.3 m past their contact stations, which forced the pelvis down every flight. A `runCycle` run bounds that overshoot to 5 % of the contact sweep by fading faster as support shortens: the sprint's 20 % support flung the swing foot low and 0.27 m past its strike, which set the whole run's carriage 13 cm lower. A squared, early-peaking sine lifts the foot and a squared sine relaxes its pitch, so both vertical and angular velocity meet the contact pose continuously.
- **Leg reach.** Before IK, the pelvis lowers as far as needed to reach the ankle targets with a soft knee (20° minimum at full locomotion amplitude). The gait hands the rig one leg's path sampled over the whole cycle (`GaitPose.stridePath`), and the pelvis is sized to that path's longest reach, not the current frame's. Sized per frame, the long run stride splayed the legs in flight and plunged the pelvis 0.3–0.5 m just when it should float: a spiky bounce. The steady carriage leaves the run's vertical motion to the flight hump and stance compression. A long stride therefore runs lower; stride length is the lever on carriage height. The path is sampled 32 times: at 16, the swing's forward peak fell between samples and the current frame's reach dipped the pelvis before every strike. The current frame's reach still applies wherever it exceeds the carriage (jumps, where the path fades out with the jump weight), eased in over 3 cm. A symmetric smooth maximum sank the pelvis whenever the swing leg merely neared its reach. This avoids straight-leg clipping and shortening the requested stride.
  - Anything that should move the pelvis vertically against a binding carriage must act after it. A dip carried in `crouch` lowers the hips the reach is measured from, and the sizing puts it back. `GaitPose.compression` is applied after the sizing.
  - `steadyCarriage` sizes the carriage from the hips without the pelvis's per-frame yaw and list, in full for a `runCycle` run and by the vault while walking. Measured from the moving hips, the carriage kinked wherever the binding sample changed. The truck's and Semi's runs keep the moving hips, whose incidental motion is part of their bounce; their `bob` sits in `crouch` and is levelled. The correction fades out with a combat overlay, which owns its own pelvis placement; the neutral exported stand is preserved.
- **Body.** Sway, hip list, hip yaw and shoulder counter-rotation are `Paced`: a `[walk, run]` pair, or one walking value the run scales itself.
  - The pelvis shifts over the planted leg and drops a little on the swing side, lagging the leg phase slightly. The earlier sway had the wrong sign and swayed over the swinging leg.
  - The heavy machines carry pelvis and chest nearly as one block while walking: list 1.5°, pelvis yaw 3°, chest counter-yaw 1.5°. At a human's 3° / 5° / 4° the pelvis rolled and twisted 9° each way under a chest held level and still, a catwalk wiggle, made worse by the old doubled cadence. Their sway (7 / 9 cm) stays: a lateral inverted pendulum over feet that wide, at that step rate, needs about 9 / 11 cm, so it reads as weight passing from foot to foot. Their running values are unchanged.
  - The hips yaw with the stepping leg; the shoulders counter-rotate and are held level against the hip list.
  - The head holds its gaze against the shoulder yaw and half of the bank.
  - The body leans with speed and into acceleration, and banks into turns.
  - Standing still, the weight shifts slowly from leg to leg.
- **Arms** follow through on damped springs (sub-stepped), with response rising within bounds as cadence increases. The default pump follows stance and recovery duration. The F1 run instead uses half-cycle opposing pumps timed to thigh drive, with compensation for the spring's phase delay. Warping each arm by a 25 % support / 75 % recovery split made both arms move forward together; a small lag cannot repair that timing.

## Steering (`game/movement.ts`)

- Camera-relative, answered at once: the turn rate asked is 9 rad/s per radian off the wanted heading (ceiling 9 rad/s walking, 6 running), taken up at 18/s, damped just under critical. Facing the other way from a stand takes about 0.4 s. The old rate (2 rad/s ceiling, 6/s response) stepped slowly round and read as unresponsive.
- Speed rises at 4.5/s and falls at 7/s; while turning, the speed kept is (cos(angle off) + 0.3) / 1.3, so a sharp turn pivots before it runs.
- The gait's stepping takes a turn on the spot as at most 2.6 m/s of motion (`TURN_STEP_MAX`): a fast pivot shuffles instead of sprinting in place. The head's turn into a turn is capped at 18°.

## Carriage per robot

Each robot's `GaitStyle` may carry its own carriage for moving, when its stand does not suit locomotion (`track`, `armAbduct`, `runElbow`, `armCross`): the feet's track and the shoulders' abduction as shares of the rig's (blended in with the stride's amplitude, standing keeps the rig's own), the elbow bend at a full run, and the upper arms' inward rotation about their own axis (applied before the swing, so the bent forearms come forward and across the body). The rig reads them from the gait pose (`GaitPose.track`, `abduct`, `armTwist`), and the fight's overlay blends from the same track.

The F1's stand is built wide (feet 0.36 m out against 0.27 m hips, arms splayed 16°). During locomotion its feet use parallel tracks at 0.82 / 0.7 of that width walking / running. Shoulder splay falls to 0.45 / 0.4, with only 6° of inward arm rotation at a run. The running elbow channel adds 52° to the rig's resting 14°, with a modest extra bend on the forward pump; total flexion stays below 95°.

The F1 is tuned against human joint ranges, because earlier passes that tuned the look directly kept reading as a comic run. Measured over the cycle, those passes had stance knees at 50° walking and 78° running (a crouched Groucho gait), feet landing far ahead with the hip never extending behind, a swing leg that trailed out straight before folding, and arms swinging mostly behind the body. The style options that fix each of these are shared, and all default to the truck's behaviour:

- `reach` (0.43 / 0.38): the share of the contact sweep ahead of the hip at the strike. A run lands closer under the body and pushes off further behind.
- `kneeFloor` (12° / 18°): the knee flexion left at the stride's longest reach, which sets the carriage height. The truck keeps 20°. The sprint's binding reach is the strike, which lands at about 20°.
- `vault` (1): the walking pelvis follows the stance leg's reach step by step. The rig fits one rise and fall per step to the cycle's reach samples, raised until every sample is reachable, so the pelvis vaults about 10 cm and is highest at mid-stance. It may rise above the stand, whose knees are bent 26°. A constant carriage with a long step forces a deep mid-stance knee instead. Running holds the constant carriage (see Leg reach).
- Walking uses `liftWindow`, raising the foot by 0.25 and lowering from 0.3 of the swing, with 0.16 m lift. Running blends into `runCycle`: a rounded 0.79 m recovery arc peaking at 0.38 of swing, then continuously descending into the forward sweep and retraction. The heel folds through behind the body before the knee comes forward. The rounded arc avoids the former high shelf and late downward stamp. `toeRelease` remains 0.5 / 0.7.
- `armCarry` (8° / 18°): the arms are carried forward against the torso lean (the stand's 8° plus the speed lean). Otherwise they hang behind a leaning chest.
- Speed lean 0 / 0.65° per m/s over the stand's 8°, so the trunk leans about 7° walking and 20° running once the pelvis, spine and chest channels are combined. Contact is 20 % of the running cycle, a sprinter's duty factor, and covers about 0.9 hip heights.
- Heel strike and toe-off at 30° / 38° walking. The strike is steeper than a human's 20° because the heel is short: 0.17 m behind the ankle, since the heel wing docks inside the foot. At 20° it lifts the ankle 4 cm, against 13 cm from the former 0.43 m heel, and the pelvis rose and fell 16 cm over 23–28° stance knees. A 30° strike landing slightly closer under the hip (`reach` 0.43) restores a 10 cm vault over 16–21° stance knees. Rolling over the sole is what lets a 0.94-hip-height step land and leave without overreaching. At 10° / 22° the ankle swept 1.14 hip heights, which left the knee at 28° through mid-stance and the pelvis rising and falling 28 cm. The run pushes off at 40°.
- `runCycle` flight is ballistic: over the flight time tf the body rises g·tf²/8 (about 5 cm at the sprint), and the stance compression g·tf·tc/2π reverses the landing's fall over the contact time tc, so height and vertical velocity are continuous. Both times are capped at 0.3 s for a slowing run, whose cycle time grows without bound. The previous 9 cm hump over a 0.12 s flight accelerated at about 5 g, and it sat on a flat stance because the compression was levelled by the reach sizing. The body was lowest at touchdown and rose through support: a hop, not a run.
- Pelvis yaw 4° / 8° and shoulders 3° / 9°: they counter-rotate more at a sprint than walking.

The truck and Semi walk with the same principles, on shorter soles: `vault` 0.5 (a full vault on the truck's short sole rose and fell 21–29 cm), heel strike and toe-off 20° / 38°, `reach` 0.45 and `kneeFloor` 12°. The stance knee holds a steady 29–36°, a heavy machine's soft carriage, and the pelvis rises and falls 8 / 13 cm. Each is a `[walk, run]` pair whose running value is the one it had.

The F1's transformation needs a knee pole tilted upward. Its moving pose blends that pole toward pelvis-forward, keeping the knees in forward-bending planes rather than kicking sideways when lifted. The locomotion pole also holds against the gait's pelvis yaw: a pole turning with the hips wagged the knees in and out, seen from behind. Reduced sway, hip list and shoulder yaw restrain the torso. These are runtime carriage changes; the transformation asset and standing pose remain authoritative.

The F1 run also owns its vertical flight independently of the lowest sole. `freeFlight` blends the final model ground correction toward a floor at the exported standing sole datum; penetration can still lift the model, but recovering feet cannot pull it down. The previous unconditional ground projection undid the rig's steady carriage and produced roughly 31 cm of final pelvis travel, even though the skeleton-only bounce check passed. Combat ownership fades this run-specific correction out, and a leap takes it over in the air (see Jumping). Walking and the truck's and Semi's runs retain their existing ground projection; the Semi's run is dragged down about 10 cm in each flight by it, the defect this fixed on the F1.

`tests/locomotion-rig.test.ts` checks full step distance, solved ankle accuracy, steady planted-foot velocity, knee range, running pelvis bounce, joint continuity through movement transitions, velocity continuity at foot contact, and the F1's knee and arm alignment and joint ranges. The F1 checks include final model ground correction, half-cycle arm opposition, a cadence within the human-similar ranges, a flight that matches gravity with the rendered bounce under 3 g at 60 Hz, and a sole roll that does not rock the body. These catch mechanical regressions; they do not establish that the assembled robot looks natural.

## Jumping

The timeline takes the forward momentum at take-off (speed over run speed). Timings (anticipation 0.36 s standing, recovery 0.42 to 0.28 s) and squat depths shorten with it. Take-off speed and gravity are the same for all: about 1.1 m high, 0.87 s in the air; momentum carries, no steering in the air, and a transformation can't start mid-jump.

**Two-footed jumps** (standing and walking, momentum up to 0.55, or a stride with no flight). The stride keeps running under the load, so feet on the ground stay put while the body loads over them; the old gait froze the stride while the body kept moving, and the feet skated through the anticipation. The feet push off their toes (staggered as the stride left them), tuck, and land side by side (`land(null)`), with the stride's footfalls silent from the loading until the landing hands back half its weight. The arms swing back during loading, forward and up before the feet leave, hold through the apex and brace into the landing. Shoulder pitch is negative forward in the authoring frame.

**The running leap is the stride itself.** It used to take the legs over: a split posed from the stride length (both legs straight, the F1's beyond its reach), the rig's reach sizing faded out with the jump's weight (the pelvis popped about 30 cm to the stand in 0.03 s and the knees locked), and on landing the phase was reset to the lead foot's strike, sweeping both feet about 3 m in opposite directions within a quarter second.

- **Take-off** is the stride's next toe-off with at least 0.08 s on that foot (`RobotGait.leapTakeoff`, which the session passes to `RobotJump.start` as the anticipation): 0.1–0.4 s after the press, against a fixed 0.14 s before. A foot must be down to push, so a press during the run's flight waits for the next one. The strikes during the load still sound.
- **Flight.** The stride's own flight, from the take-off leg's toe-off to the lead foot's strike, is stretched over the jump's (`leapWarp`). The legs leave and land at the stride's rate and slow to 15 % of the mean between, so the pose holds. At the apex they gather (`jump.tuck`): the lead foot is raised by half the style's `jumpTuck` and drawn 30 % toward the hips (knee up), and the trailing foot is raised by 0.9 `jumpTuck` (heel folded). The arms run on with the warped stride: the arm opposite the lead leg is held forward.
- **Carriage.** The leap keeps the stride's path, knee floor and track, so the pelvis stays on the run's carriage. The load's crouch and the landing's sink are `compression`, after the reach sizing. The body owns its height in the air (`freeFlight`), so gathering the legs does not move it.
- **Landing.** The lead foot lands as the stride's strike (`land(lead)`), with the trailing leg still swinging through, and the stride runs on. The sink (0.8 `jumpCrouch`) starts at the touchdown speed as a quarter sine and recovers over 0.22 s. It is firm (7–11 g stopping 5.2 m/s), but it has no jolt: stopping dead and then crouching measured 47–81 g.

All pose channels stay continuous through take-off and touchdown at standing, walking and running speed (tested against rate bounds that catch one-frame resets). `tests/leap.test.ts` presses the jump at four stride phases on each robot and checks the take-off foot, the load's carriage, joint rates, the knees in the held leap, the strike at touchdown and the landing's acceleration. `node tools/gait-probe.mjs --jump [--table]` shows a jump frame by frame.

## On the ground's relief

Once the car form is half gone, the robot stands at the ground's height under its standing point (`game/ground-follow.ts`), eased while it transforms and exact once it stands; a jump's height is added to it. It stays upright on a slope: the car's tilt fades out over the first half of the transformation. There is no foot IK. The feet are placed relative to the body's footing, so on the steeper dune faces a foot can sink or float by a few centimetres over a stride. The fortress, where the fighting is, is level.
