# Impala character

The 1967 Impala, its robot (5.47 m, the pickup robot's size) and the transformation are authored in Blender (`blender/impala_build/`, impala-model.md) and exported as `assets/impala.{json,bin}`; the cutlass is `assets/impala-cutlass.{json,bin}` (weapons.md). The package is `src/content/impala/`. The exported model is the authority for everything in game: proportions, slots and colours come from the build (`kit.py` `materials()`).

## Runtime choices

- **Parts on the live skeleton** (`CARRIED`, `BRACES`, transformation-mechanics.md): the exhaust tails, the rear quarters, the rear axle stubs and the cowl vents ride the bones they rest on, not the ones the bake hangs them from; the door, spine, roof, neck and head braces are laid between their two carriers every frame. Before, a raised arm swung the quarters and exhaust tails off into the air and left the door braces hanging.
- **Feet:** the foot and toe castings are `part:` nodes (the bones carry no geometry), so `footNodes` names `part:foot.*` / `part:toe.*`.
- **Soles** (fitted with `gait-probe.mjs --sole`): toe 0.65, heel 0.19, rounded 9 cm, ankle 0.417. The heel pad stands up to 9 mm higher than the toe pad (the bottom rakes about a degree), levelled by `soleTilt` -1; with it the walk's foot-flat stays under 3 g at 60 Hz (asset test).
- **Gait:** the heavy machine scaled to 2.87 m hips (walk 3.77 m/s on 2.27 m steps, Froude 0.48; run 14.5 m/s on 4.26 m steps), the stand's wide feet (0.94 m out against 0.47 m hips) drawn in while moving. The run is a `runCycle` run: its flight is ballistic (1.4 g at 60 Hz), and the ground projection leaves the body its height while both feet are up.
- **Hands** (rig.ts `HandLayout`): the knuckle row runs across the hand (the fingers flex about X), the thumb sits on the opposite side of the row from the finger named `index`, and each forearm turns in 32 degrees about its own axis in the stand. The rig reads all three from the bones: the gait keeps the turn and flexes each finger by its own stand angle, so the handover from the baked timeline is seamless (the asset test checks the stand to 1e-4).
- **Profile** (`IMPALA_PROFILE`): measured from the car (3.14 m wheelbase, 1.68 m track, 0.36 m tyres, the front axle 1.61 m ahead of the origin). A heavy, soft cruiser: low grip that gives up early (1.02 at 0.16 rad), all drive at the rear and a loose drift rear (0.64), 15 m/s² drum brakes, more roll and pitch than the others (1.15 Hz ride), 42 / 54 m/s.
- **Materials** (`materials.ts`): every slot at the build's linear colour, metalness, roughness and coat. The Tuxedo black lacquer carries a little orange peel in its coat's roughness; chrome a faint wiping. Lamps are tungsten-warm emissives (`IMPALA_LIGHTS`: the headlamps dim to the chest's share in robot form, the tail lamps brighten braking, the markers blink while it transforms). The eyes are the build's blue and blaze with `eyeBoost`.
- **Glass** is opaque, dark and clear-coated, as on every car: blended panes are reactive coverage to the temporal resolve (rendering-and-boot.md) and shimmer at windscreen size. The cabin trim behind it is hidden.
- **Effects** (`effects.ts`): a tyre leaving or meeting the sand during the transformation throws dust, read from the posed wheel contacts, so it holds both ways without authored events.

## Audio (`audio/`)

- **Engine** (`big-block.ts`): fitted to `ref_sounds/67_impala_engine.mp3` (a pull through the gears) by `tools/engine-model.mjs`, which writes `engine-model.ts`: at low revs (about 2200 rpm) and high (about 5000) the engine orders (harmonics of rpm / 120, relative to the firing order) and the noise between them (its band shape, its RMS against the orders', how deeply it pulses at the firing rate). The voice plays each timbre's orders as a periodic wave at measured amplitude (with a second bank a few cents off), crossfaded by revs, and its noise as a seamless shaped loop (`shapedNoise`, frequency-domain synthesis) played at the revs' ratio to the timbre's, pulsed at the firing rate. The analysis windows are short (85 ms at high revs), so the pull's rising revs keep the orders apart from the noise. Its output level sits about 2 dB over the Bat's V8 in the mix.
  - **Chew:** no two firings alike. A strictly periodic wave of the measured orders read smooth and synthetic; a real big-block's pulses land a little stronger or weaker each time (cycle-to-cycle combustion variation, widest with a big cam at low revs and light load) over the cross-plane crank's uneven bank rhythm (1-8-4-3-6-5-7-2: L R R L R L L R). `firingChew` writes one level per firing (a near-normal spread plus its bank's lean, eased between firings, mean zero, held within 2.4 deviations so the gain never goes negative) as a seamless 64-cycle loop, played in step with the revs into a gain on everything the engine sounds: 30% deep at low revs off the throttle, tightening to 13% as it spins up and pulls.
  - **Rumble:** voiced over the measurement, which is a distant pass: the half orders under the firing order (the burble) at 1.9 times their measured amplitude, and the exhaust's low resonance as a +5 dB shelf under 150 Hz.
- **Gearbox:** a three-speed automatic, 2.48 / 1.48 / 1.00 (the recording's shift drops the revs by about 1.45, its 2-3), shift points rising with throttle, kickdown.
- **Transformation:** the shared machine in a body of large thin pressed-steel panels (modes ringing longer than an armoured body's). **The fight sounds only the swing, the bite and the robot's footfalls.** The shared sounds layered under the truck's blows read as a steam leak under every move: the joint drive (`servo`, the machine's whirr), `slam` (the truck's axe into the ground, its sand hissing as it rains back for over a second, which the Impala's stamps, push-offs and landings had played) and the pass-by rush on the rising cut and the cyclone. The Impala's moves cue no `servo`; its stamps, push-offs and landings play its own footfall, heavy; the cleave's point in the sand is its own `bite` (the sand and sparks thrown up, a thud). The special sounds none of them either (no `slam`, the pass-by rush or the `sizzle` as the blade comes out of the sand): its weight is the robot's own heavy footfall (`ThunderParts.thud`) under its explosions and lightning. **Tyres:** narrow bias-ply, higher and thinner squeal.
- **The cutlass's bite** is the soldiers' `cutlass` blow (enemies.md), fitted to `ref_sounds/cutlass_hit.mp3` taken whole from its onset (it is all bite: a 150-250 Hz thump as the blade lands, then a dense tearing through the whole spectrum over a heavy low body for half a second, then faint high lines ringing out past a second); the take is 1.3 s. Its envelope fluctuation and cross-band structure measure as the recording's without extra modulation: narrow-band noise under the fitted envelope already flutters as a tear does.
- **The cutlass's swing** is the racer's sword swing (`SWORD_SWING`, the shared swing voice), its full-swing speed scaled for the cutlass's longer edge (29 m/s against the longsword's 24: the edge runs about a fifth faster through the same swing). Its own tuning, between the truck's and the racer's, roared like the truck's.
- **Lightning** (`combat/audio/lightning.ts`): fitted to `ref_sounds/lightening.mp3`, a sustained arc with no thunder. A dense train of small discharges (3000 a second, lognormal sizes, each dying in a couple of milliseconds) over shaped noise, swelling over tens of ms, with a steady hiss above 3 kHz: measured band kurtosis about 6 (the recording 5.5-8), the 5 ms envelope's 10-90% spread 8.2 dB (8.7), about 15 standouts a second 8 dB over the rest (11). The first fit (700 large discharges a second) measured twice the kurtosis and six times the standouts: popping rather than an arc's sizzle. A strike flares a stretch of the loop; the discharge's arc voice plays it on.

## Effects (`combat/fx/`)

- **Camera-facing quads must face the camera.** The bolts' and sparks' quads are built across their axis from `cross(toward the quad from the camera, axis)`; the other order winds them away from the camera, every quad is back-face culled and the effect never draws, whatever its shading. The lightning and the shared sparks had it backwards from their first pass (`node tools/facing-probe.mjs` counts each effect's lit pixels alone in an empty scene: 0 is culled).
- **Blade arcs** (`blade-arcs.ts`): the heavy cuts' light, a thick crescent recorded from the edge between `arc` cues. A cut moves the tip metres in a frame, so the path between frames is rebuilt along the blade's own turn (its direction slerped about the turn's axis, the grip carried between, a sample every 2.6 degrees or 0.3 m of tip, at most 24 a frame): sampled once a frame, the arc was a bent polyline of chords. The band covers the blade from 30% of its length to a little past its point: a white-hot line along the point's path over a broad crimson body that is eaten from the inside toward that line as each sample ages, so the arc thins toward its tail; gone within 0.36 s. 192 samples an arc holds a full turn; an arc that fills runs on into the next unbroken. Each arc written in a frame is finished and uploaded once (tested).
- **Full circle** (`sweep-ring.ts`): the finale's 360-degree cut of light: a flat annulus at the blade's height swept round from the robot's left in the swing's sense in 0.12 s (the effects' clock: the blow's slow motion stretches it), the band from the fist out past the point, rolling 12 m out with the wave, the same light as the blade arcs. Drawn by uniforms (one at a time), nothing uploads.
- **Lightning** (`lightning.ts`): bolts by midpoint displacement with forks, camera-facing segment quads in one instanced additive draw (8192 segments; the discharge keeps about 4000 alive), a return-stroke flash, a stepped current flicker. Generation is allocation-free; a strike uploads its run of the ring in at most two ranges (tested). A crawling bolt keeps to the ground a few centimetres over it. The discharge (special-fx.ts) bursts out all round within a second (26 m/s to 24 m, 18 main channels re-struck every 70 ms, 0.7-0.8 m wide), as the reference's screen-spanning white-cored bolts do.
- **Shock ring** (`shock-ring.ts`): a band of crimson light expanding round the robot, eased out, pooled as three instances.
- **Lights:** the forming weapon's and the blast's (the fighter's), and the discharge's (`ThunderFx`), the third slot.

## Combat animation (`combat/pose.ts`, `moves.ts`, `special.ts`)

- **Posed as a person, carried over to the robot.** The user rejected the first two passes as tip-toe sword dancing: motion kept small to keep the robot's parts apart. Moves are authored as a human fighter would move with a heavy one-handed blade (long strides that land with the blade, the hips dropping and turning first, the chest after, the arm and blade last and fastest, the weight over the front knee, the free arm thrown against the blow, follow-through well past the target), accepting small overlaps of the robot's parts where they are not obvious.
- **The grip decides the cuts.** The cutlass's grip runs across the fist, so the blade leaves the thumb's side square to the forearm (within the wrist's 50 degrees), its edge along the forearm. Its natural direction depends on the arm: held low it points ahead and in across the body; raised over the shoulder it lies back behind it; a fist in front at the chest stands it up. Blades pointed straight ahead of a fist at the chest, or out to the left, can only be had by wringing the hand half round on the forearm: the first passes asked for them everywhere (measured 150-180 degrees through the combo) and read as a twisted arm. Cuts are an axe's in one hand: back over the shoulder on the wind-up, over the top, down and through as the forearm drives down; the rising cut scoops the blade up with the arm.
- **Natural hold** (`CombatBuild.natural`, combat.md; its stability rules there): the overlay solves the cutlass arm every frame from the real shoulder: the elbow takes the roll that leaves the hand least turned on its forearm (within 85 degrees of the stand's hand), not winging up while the hand is low (22), not tucking inside the shoulder (the car's front end stands out of the chest: tucked elbows ran the upper arm into the hood, nose and bumper), near the last frame's roll; the blade then gives where the wish would wring the hand or bend the wrist. An authoring-time estimate of the arm missed by 70-130 degrees once the torso turned, pitched and bent hard (the shoulder rides on hips, spine and chest at different heights), so the solve lives at runtime and holds between keys too.
- **Blade wishes the arm can hold.** A wish nearly along the forearm (the blade straight up on a raised arm, or pointed back along it) leaves where the blade goes round the forearm to the clamp, and transitions through such wishes jerked it: author the wish near what the arm holds there (`clash-probe` and a held-against-wish print show it), and pass the blade over the top with an upright key between two sides half a turn apart in yaw.
- **Cuts are keyed along their arcs** (`Timeline.cut`): channels are monotone per axis, so a swing keyed at its ends carried the fist on near-straight lines between them. A cut samples splines through its path frames every 24 ms, the hips and legs leading (`lead`), the arm and blade lagging (`lag`).
- **Flights are ballistic** (`flight`): `air` on a parabola keyed finely enough to follow it. The special's first leap rose 3.1 m and covered 9 m in 0.38 s (17 g) and read as the robot moved there; a 2.3 m, 0.7 s leap after it read as a hop, and two bounding strides as running. What the user wanted is one step driven straight into the crowd on a nearly level line (Black Thunder below): a dash is not a thrown body, so its `air` is lifted at once, held level and set down, not a parabola. The cyclone flies 0.5 s at 1.9 m (about 6 g).
- **Swings go toward the blade's side.** The finale is held as the user's reference holds it, mirrored for the left hand: the cutlass laid across the chest, the fist before the right shoulder (well out ahead of the car's front) and the blade flat back past it, the body wound right; then swung right to left, the whole left side open to the follow-through. Swung left to right from the left shoulder, the body was in the blade's way. The swing is a flat backhand (below).
- **Spins go toward the blade's side.** Holding the cutlass in the left hand, a spin turns to the left (anticlockwise seen from above): the turn opens room on that side, and the blow is a backhand whip the turn carries, not an arm held out while the body turns (the first cyclone, turning right). The body winds right with the arm across the front; the hips fire first, the chest unwinds after them, the arm lashes out from across the body to full stretch on the left at the blow and wraps on round behind, the free arm pulled in. The blade goes round about 100 degrees more than the body (measured 460 to 360 in the cyclone), its speed rising to the blow and falling off after it.
- **The charge lands straight into the flip.** No separate whirl: as the charge's lead foot meets the sand the body drops into a deep wide crouch, sliding on (the left foot out some 2 m to the side, the right drawn in under the bent knee), and the blade sweeps up out of the lance through the front and on overhead, the rising cut that throws everything round it into the sky. It holds the whole body's pose while they fly (`LANDED`), the camera staying on it, pushing in, a little slower: the weight over the bent right knee, the hips shifted and tipped right, the torso leaning to the right hand reaching down, the sword arm flung straight up and out with the blade on along it, tipped a little forward (`FLUNG`: the wrist at its limit and the grip pivoted in the fist; tipped any other way the hand turned on the forearm or let the blade fall level). reachOut's shoulder misses the crouch's pitched, tilted one, so the blade is laid along the arm where the arm really points (`ARM_SAG`). An arm raised alone over a standing body read as a twisted arm and not as a pose. The rising cut carries the blade up the front with the arm and lets it fall out to the left only at the top: carried out ahead of the arm, it wrung the hand. The charge starts later than the leap did (0.78 s, the gather held on the face), so the bodies are thrown at much the same time and the lightning still catches them high.
- **Every change of grip is a throw.** Each pose's grip is the one a person would hold it in, and between grips the cutlass is thrown (`toss`, the overlay's `w.free`): its centre of mass on a parabola under gravity, turning at one rate, the way round that keeps the blade outside the arm; the open hand meets it. A throw is a plain toss and regrip, as a person does it: a short whip, a flight of about 0.45 s, the shortest turn between the two holds and no extra whole turns (`turns` 0; whole extra turns read as juggling, the user's rejection), the open hand following just under the hilt. The flung arm's ordinary grip cannot point a blade down from overhead, so the arm is drawn in, the fist before the face with the blade standing up (a fist before the face is the one place the ordinary grip stands a blade up: with the arm still flung up the forearm points up too, and a blade stood up along it spins the hand), and popped straight up and let go early in the drive, before the forearm stands up under the blade; it turns over once (about 140 degrees) outside the arm, the point swinging out and down and the hilt coming up, rises some 0.6 m and is taken just past the top, reversed, point down (`RAISED`, the reference's raise): the arm straight up over the shoulder, the blade hanging out and down beside the head (hung steeper, its root lay against the forearm's door plate). Torn out of the sand, the reversed grip whips the blade out to the left and lets it go: it turns on up outside the arm and is taken in the ordinary grip high on the left, lying back over the head (let go hanging under the fist, it had to turn up through the hand). The release and catch placements are the holds' own, measured (`AUDIT_PLACE`; impala.test fails when a hold changes and they drift).
- **The stab and the haul are the reversed grip's.** The stab swings the long arm over and down from the shoulder, the blade kept point down (folding the elbow under the fist stood the forearm up, where no grip points a blade down it). Over the lightning and through the haul the fist sits on top of the hilt, the forearm about level and the elbow down; the haul curls it up.
- **Down and across, the blade lowered with the arm.** From the catch the arm sweeps down in front of the face to the right shoulder and the blade, lying back over the head, lowers with it until it lies flat back past the right shoulder: swung down through the front as the reference does, it wrung the hand and threw the elbow over the top.
- **The finale is a flat backhand.** Palm down throughout, so the blade stays level: trailing the fist through the front, then snapping into line with the arm as the follow-through throws the whole arm straight out on the left, carried a little past the shoulder's line toward the back (`SWUNG`, `OUT`: the grip pivoted in the fist as on the flung arm, the blade tipped 10 degrees off the arm's line; laid exactly on it, the blade drooped and the hand wound round the forearm), held there. The free arm comes round in front of the body with the hips and hangs bent before it: flung out behind, it bent behind the back and through the rear fender. From the follow-through the arm comes back round ahead on the left with the elbow giving before it is lowered: lowered straight from behind, the elbow and the hand flipped over to bring the blade ahead. Lowered after it, the blade settles level ahead (the lowered hand's own blade): turned in across the body or down along the hanging forearm, it rolled the hand over.
- **The finale's held edge faces ahead.** The edge faces along the forearm, so the hold across the chest reaches nearly straight across the front at chest height (the wind eased to -22 so the left shoulder stays back): with the elbow low the forearm stood up, turned the edge up and raised the knuckle bow over the face.
- **Black Thunder opens with a one-step charge**: driven off the back foot, 15.5 m in 0.48 s (some 32 m/s) on a level line 0.35 m over the sand (the pelvis holds within 0.1 m), the body pitched hard into it, the lead leg reaching ahead and the trailing leg stretched back, the blade levelled ahead like a lance; it lands sliding 0.8 m into the flip, both feet ploughing. The sand breaks under the push-off (`break`) and is ploughed by the skid (`skid`), with sand torn up and the air shoved ahead while it runs (`rush`). It bowls through the soldiers in its path (hits.ts). The blade carried on the shoulder through the charge passed through the legs (trailing low) or forced the elbow round the arm on the way into the next blow; levelled ahead it is the arm's own low hold and runs straight up into the flip's rising cut.
- **The planted hilt stays where it is planted.** 2.3 m ahead of the pelvis from the stab to the pull (`PLANTED_AT`): the blade is in the sand, so the body moves round it, not it with the body. At the plant the body sinks at the knees rather than pitching over it (pitched, the shoulder came 0.8 m forward over the hilt and folded the arm).
