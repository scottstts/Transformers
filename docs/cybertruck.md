# Cybertruck character

The vehicle is authored in a shared local frame: +Z forward, +Y up, +X right, in metres. The humanoid stands about `ROBOT_Z` forward of the vehicle origin. The transform path runs from 0 (vehicle) to 1 (robot); reverse transformation traverses the same path backward. The 8-second duration is the canonical value in the character choreography.

Panels begin in exact vehicle positions. The model maps each panel into a skeleton bone's folded frame, then applies its hinge, slide, and unlatch steps over the authored time windows. The track evaluator limits tangents so interpolated joints do not overshoot. Ground lift is solved from support points for intermediate and robot poses; tyres stay at their radius while vehicle suspension moves the body.

The robot's gait hands over at progress 1. While transforming, either locomotion path brakes toward a stop. A transform request made while moving waits until speed is below 0.3 m/s; R may reverse an in-progress transform. The car uses direct throttle, brake, reverse, and steering inputs with bicycle yaw, independent of camera orientation. Robot movement remains camera-relative and pivots around the robot standing point. Motion values in `src/game/movement.ts` are the defaults for this character until another vehicle needs its own tuning.

Click to enter with pointer lock. Once locked, move the mouse to look, W/S or up/down to drive forward or brake and reverse, A/D or left/right to steer, R to transform, and Shift to boost or run. The chase camera looks along +Z, where its screen-right direction is -X, so car steering maps right input to negative local yaw. In robot form, WASD and arrow keys move in camera space. Escape releases pointer lock; click the game to reacquire it. Movement keys are cleared when lock is lost. Normal play has no on-screen controls or HUD.

The camera orbits at a stable radius around its smoothed focus point: 7.875 m in car form and 12.75 m in robot form. The radius eases between those fixed distances during transformation. Player zoom is disabled. A pitch limit keeps the camera above the ground without shortening the radius. While car driving keys are held, the camera smoothly returns behind the vehicle after 0.3 seconds without mouse movement. Mouse movement takes control immediately; releasing the driving keys leaves the chosen view in place.

The character's visible materials are node materials and TSL. Geometry remains authored as named panels and bone structure so moving parts retain their identities. Altering panel geometry or transform steps should be checked across both endpoints and intermediate poses, not just in one form.
