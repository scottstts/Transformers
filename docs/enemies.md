# Enemy soldiers

Each district's commander (a 7 m unit with a lance combo that can knock the robot back) is commander.md; it shares the soldier's body code (`Commander extends Soldier`) and the horde's hits, rays, aim assist, bars and warm-up. How enemies take blows is the combat contract (combat.md): a soldier's reactions for every enemy, and an enemy mid-combo interrupted only by a special.

Every district of the citadel (citadel.md) keeps a garrison of 3 m wheeled robot soldiers. The game side is `src/game/enemies/`: `horde.ts` (the update, hits, culling and drawing), `garrison.ts` (a district's garrison: posts, reinforcements, alert and stand-down, patrols), `engage.ts` (the fight round the robot), `navigation.ts` (way-finding), `soldier.ts` (one body's physics, health, reactions and pose), `debris.ts` (the break-apart) and `barrier.ts` (the car ring). The soldier's asset, rig, poses, materials, renderer, health bars and sound are `src/content/soldier/`; its Blender build is soldier-model.md.

## Rendering the horde

- The whole horde is a fixed number of draws, whatever its size: one mesh per material slot per detail tier, plus two shadow proxies. Every vertex carries its bone index; the renderer keeps an affine 3x4 matrix per bone per drawn soldier and one vec4 of state per soldier (heat, dissolve, lights, blade). The TSL vertex stage skins rigid parts from them (`materials.ts`). Previous bone/state buffers and a prior-slot lookup preserve each body's motion when sorting and LOD change its packed index; new bodies use current positions as their first history. Normals follow by assigning `normalLocal` in the position node.
- The tiers are distance bands (`LOD_DISTANCE`: 26 / 70 m), capped at 18 soldiers in LOD0 and 48 in LOD1 (`LOD_CAP`): a crowd pressing round the robot would otherwise put ~85k triangles each on screen. `Horde.drawFor` culls soldiers to the frustum on the CPU, sorts them near to far and hands the list over; since the tiers are bands of a sorted list (and the caps only push the farther ones down a tier), each tier is one consecutive run of the buffers. One material per slot serves all tiers: a per-mesh `userData.base` (read with `onObjectUpdate`) offsets the instance index. Capacity is 320 drawn at once (`HORDE_CAPACITY`; per-instance buffers only, the LOD caps bound the detail); the citadel has 368 garrison soldiers, distributed across twelve districts.
- Posing the rig (20 bone matrices) is a soldier's costliest step, so it is deferred: `Soldier.update` eases the channels and places the body, and `refresh()` poses the rig only for soldiers drawn this frame or read (a blow's sparks, a blade's reach, the break-up).
- Only the used prefix of each buffer is uploaded (`addUpdateRange`).
- Shadows: the lit meshes cast none. Two slot-less proxies live on `SHADOW_ONLY_LAYER` (`rendering/layers.ts`), which the sun's shadow cascades enable and the view camera doesn't: the LOD2 proxy for the nearest (within 32 m, at most 20: a prefix of the sorted list) and a proxy of one box per part (the debris boxes, ~240 triangles, no blade) for every other soldier out to the far cascade (150 m). Soldiers just off screen whose shadows can reach into view (7 m) are packed after the visible ones and only cast. Every soldier in range has a shadow: a 42 m cut-off made them pop in. Both proxies shrink parts toward their bone origin as they dissolve, so shadows don't outlive the burning debris.
- A destroyed soldier is the same instance: the debris writes its bones' matrices into its rig.
- Keep CPU culling generous for debris (7 m sphere), which scatters from the body.

## Behaviour

- **Alert and stand-down** (`garrison.ts` `updateAlert`): a district's garrison is alerted when the robot comes into the district. It follows the robot one district over (through the gate), and stands down once the robot has been out of the fortress, or further than that, for 1 s (`CALL_OFF`): back to its beats from wherever the fight left it, finding its way through the gates. Before, a fort stood down only 25 m beyond its car barrier, so after leaving the soldiers crowded the inside of the gate for a long time trying to reach the robot. The 1 s keeps a body stepping through a gate's opening from flickering it. Starting an alert needs the robot `present`; staying alerted doesn't (a special or a jump doesn't call them off).
- **At peace** each walks its post's beat (`Post.beat` in plan.ts), rolling slowly (1.8 m/s) to each point, stopping there 0.6-2.8 s and looking about: patrols circle the district's yard (at least 45 % of the garrison, alternate ones the other way round), sentries pace along the wall beside each of its gates (2.2-6 s stops), a guard paces before each spawn door. A garrison standing still at its posts looked dead. Beat points stand clear of every collider and inside their own district (tested); the planner pushes a point clear or drops it.
- **The ring**: the nearest `RING` (9) close in at the robot's body radius + 1.55 m (or just outside its raised shield, `EnemyTarget.guard`); the rest hold 5 m further out, drifting round. The fighting ring leans 30 % of the way round toward the robot's heading. Without that, soldiers stayed wherever they arrived from, and a robot facing the emptiest side missed whole combos.
- **Attacks**: at most `ATTACKERS` (4) swing at once, counted across every alerted garrison (they share one target and one ring). A slash lands at a fixed moment of its strike; it hits if the robot is within reach and inside a 60 degree cone of the soldier's heading. Soldiers only reach the robot while it is `present` (not in a special's cutscene, not airborne). A soldier that takes a blow doesn't swing for 1.2 s (`REEL`): a combo's target reels rather than trades blows.
- **Way-finding** (`navigation.ts` `CitadelNav`): between districts a soldier follows the plan's shortest gate route, never through the outside unless leaving: its own-side waypoint, then the far one. Crossing remains committed by the body's place in the gate lane. Within a district the flow field stays a 2 m, eight-way Dijkstra grid with no corner cutting and the established three-cell lookahead. Fields cover only a gate's joined districts or a yard's own district, plus a margin, rather than the full kilometre-wide site. Both body sizes build every field at boot; first-use construction during combat would hitch. Gate-side normalization and polygon nesting are explained in citadel.md.
- **Floors**: body height and jump velocity stay local to the current floor. A changed floor updates placement and pose immediately. Hits, pulls, assist and body interactions require floor separation ≤ 2.5 m, preventing adjacent tiers from fighting through retaining walls; same-floor timing, damage and reactions are unchanged. The session updates target floor before a hit, including the first blow after moving onto the crown.
- **Wheels**: soldiers roll. Near their point they shuffle holonomically (each foot steers its wheels toward the motion); far away they face the way they roll. Coasting has low friction, sideways skidding high. After a blow they brake hard (`SOLDIER.brake`): free-rolling knock-back carried them ~20 m and scattered the crowd out of reach.
- **Aim assist**: `RobotCombat.aimAssist` turns a move toward the nearest standing soldier within 7 m and 0.9 rad of the camera's heading. It uses the same re-aim limit as the camera aim.

## Hits and reactions

- **Health**: a soldier has 300 (`SOLDIER.health`). The combos' first three blows (255 truck, 245 racer, 258 Bat) leave one standing and the finisher takes it down, so every blow reads as a bite, not a kill. A blow takes its damage off; only a blow that empties it destroys it.
- **The flinch**: a blow that doesn't throw it snaps the body into a hit pose (`POSES.hitHigh`, snapped back with the head thrown and a forearm over the face; `POSES.hitLow`, doubled over), alternating blow by blow so a combo rocks it back and forth; it holds 0.3-0.5 s (longer for harder blows) and eases back into its stance. The snap is fast (rate 34), the return the stance's own ease. The direction springs ride on top.
- **The special holds its kills**: a special's blows before its last (`HitEvent.final` false) cannot destroy; a soldier they empty is `doomed`, held in its flinch (trembling) or lying where it fell, never getting up or fighting again. The special's last blow destroys what it empties and then breaks every doomed soldier (`Horde.settle`), wherever it stands. While a special plays (`Horde.special`) walls and bowling bodies don't destroy either; the session settles again as the cutscene ends, for anything the last blow missed. `final` is the special's latest strike or blast (`lastHitTime`).
- **Health bars** (`content/soldier/health-bars.ts`): one instanced draw of camera-facing bars over the heads of the drawn living soldiers within 62 m (fading from 46 m), world size up to 24 m away and constant on screen beyond. A dark glass plate leaning like the energy meter's cells, the fill, a pale chip trailing the last blows (it holds 0.4 s, then drains), quarter ticks and a hit flash, all shaped in the fragment stage and footprint-filtered. Depth-tested: walls hide the bars behind them. Levels stay under the bloom threshold.
- The robots' blows are authored per move as ground-plane volumes (`transformer/combat/hits.ts`): sector strikes, sweeps along a move's path, and radial blasts. A robot's fist or edge path would sail over a 3 m soldier's head, so contact is not traced from limbs.
- A sweep hits each soldier once (a serial per sweep), throwing it along the motion and out of the path. Sweep knock is capped at 16 m/s.
- **Flash Move** follows rule 7 of the shared combat contract: a broad moving front catches the crowd independently of player mesh contact, giving varied forward kicks, lift and a small outward spread. Bodies abruptly lose footing and stay tipped with loose limbs through descent; only landing starts their catch. Existing momentum and collision response decide travel, without preset endpoints or a forced stop at recovery. Weight scales the shared response, so soldiers fly farther and pitch more than commanders. It causes no damage, including secondary wall/body contacts during the response; bodies bowled on inherit that harmless response. It uses no combat launch/down/get-up sequence. A commander mid-combo retains its protection; existing falls and special holds keep ownership.
- **A vacuum** (a move's pull, `Soldier.pull`): a soldier within its reach staggers (it cannot drive against it) and slides toward the centre on its wheels at up to the pull's speed, slowing as it arrives (1.6 m/s per metre from the centre); one thrown into the air drifts that way as it falls. Held in it, it seizes as a flurry's blows make it: thrown between its two hit poses on a jittered 0.1 s beat, each beat a flurry blow's jolt back away from the draw (head up), shaking, and on for 0.25 s after the draw lets go (the blow's spring kick is shared, `Soldier.jolt`). Only leaning in the ready pose, it read as drifting, not caught. The crowd gathers where the blow will land instead of overshooting it; the soldiers' own collisions keep them from stacking.
- Reactions come from physics plus pose springs. The push goes into the wheel velocity. Past `launchLift` or `launchKnock` the soldier flies ballistically, tumbling about the axis across the push. The tumble rate is set from the flight time so it turns about a third of a revolution and lands on its back or face; a fixed rate spun it upright again. Landed upright it staggers; otherwise it lies 1.1-1.9 s and gets up.
- The fight's dust supports the blows rather than filling the air: a soldier thrown hard scuffs a little sand (4 puffs past a knock of 9), a break-up 7, and the world budgets bursts (worlds.md).
- Bodies bowl each other over: a collision faster than 5 m/s between a flying and a standing soldier shares momentum as an impact. A body thrown into a wall faster than 7 m/s takes damage.
- A connected strike adds a short hit-stop in the session.

## Breaking apart (`debris.ts`)

- Every part is a rigid box (its bone's box and mass from the export). Pieces fly off with the blow, a scatter out from the chest and a tumble. Ground contact is an impulse at the deepest box corner with full inertia.
- Friction must also act on resting contact, bounded by the weight the piece bears. With friction only on impact, pieces slid like ice. Lying pieces plough the sand, which damps their motion.
- Pieces don't collide with each other or with the walls.
- They lie 4 s, then burn away over 1 s (the materials' noise dissolve with a blackbody edge), 5 s in all. Heat glow on whole parts is for blasts only; any heat on cuts read as red-hot parts.

## Reinforcements

Garrison sizes come from the exported district plan (368 in all, crown 44; `DISTRICTS` in blender/citadel_build/ctd/plan.py). When a fight cuts one below 55 %, a wave rolls out of its district's spawn bays in turn, one soldier every 0.6 s until it is whole again. At peace it refills one every 3 s. These reinforcement timings and thresholds retain the established combat behavior.

## Simulation cost

Districts tick by distance from the camera (to their bounds): every frame within 90 m or while fighting, every other frame within 200 m, every sixth beyond, not at all past 460 m. Static collision goes through the citadel's `ColliderGrid`; soldier-soldier collision is one pass over every soldier stepped that frame (districts share bodies at their gates).

## Fighting feel

The target is a musou brawler (One Piece: Pirate Warriors 3): crowds go down within a combo and fly, each blow visibly rocks them, the robot answers the keys at once. A combo's finisher takes a soldier down (see Health). The robots' blows are wide and long (arcs 95-180°, reach 3.5-5.8 m) and throw hard enough that finishers launch. Controls follow the shared contract in combat.md (fresh window clicks, immediate guard, Flash Move, steering each move and movement cutting the recovery) and robot-locomotion.md (steering).

## Sound (`content/soldier/audio.ts`)

- Two shared continuous voices follow the whole horde's distance-weighted activity: wheels on sand, and the plasma blades. A voice per soldier would cost too much, and identical loops read as synthetic.
- Events are one-shots capped at 6 per frame, delayed and dulled by distance.
- The soldiers are small machines heard under the robot's own sound: quiet, close-up textures (a soft sand crunch, a faint electrical crackle), levels saturating with the crowd (a horde is a denser texture, not a louder one), at most 4 events a frame.
- No `roar` texture: it is a jet's roar, and the blades' loop built on it, summed over the horde with a loud wheel rumble, sounded like a turbine next to the player.
- Nothing is pitched and no filter moves: every filter is fixed and wide, only levels are shaped. The first voice used the `chatter` texture (clicks that ring resonators at random pitches) for the wheels' grit and swept noise bands for swings, ignitions and impacts; a crowd of them read as robot "beep-boop" babble. The guard's sounds (heard when soldiers strike) follow the same rule, and so do the robots' debris sprinkles. The one exception is the breakup's metal (below).
- **Breaking apart and parts landing** should sound like light, hollow, thin-walled metal: an empty soda can dropped, not heavy plate. The first version (a crunching tear, then randomly timed low thuds) was rejected.
  - `debris.ts` reports each part's real touchdowns (`Debris.landings`: which piece and how fast; faster than 0.9 m/s, and at most once per 0.08 s per part), so bounces and tumbles are heard when they happen and parts at rest are silent.
  - Each touchdown (`SoldierAudio.land`) plays a pre-rendered strike from `can-bank.ts`: a dense cluster of close, beating shell modes excited by a noisy micro-contact crunch, plus a weak hollow body mode. A first version built from a few clean sine partials sounded cartoonish. A recording of a real can drop was the reference for its character, not for exact matching. A hard landing adds one or two lighter re-strikes as the part rocks. Each part keeps one shell, played at a fixed rate by size (smaller is higher).
  - Speed sets the level; mass only shades it (a heavy torso still sounds light and hollow). Distance lowers and dulls it with a fixed low-pass. The bank (6 shells × 4 strikes) and the blow takes render during entry loading. If audio was unavailable then, they retain the one-take-per-frame fallback.
  - A budget of 24 landings a second (bursts of 8) keeps a blast's shower a light patter. Landings beyond 70 m are not played.
  - The breakup itself is a light metallic crinkle with a soft low push and a couple of ticks.
- **The blows** (`hit-bank.ts`, `hit-models.ts`, `SoldierAudio.blow`): each robot blow that catches soldiers sounds once (not per soldier; more soldiers make it fuller, `log2` of the count), a sweep catching soldiers frame after frame at most every 70 ms. Four kinds, each modelled on a Foley recording the user supplied: *punch* for blunt blows, *heavy* (the stomp) for blasts and blows that throw (knock >= 13 or lift >= 3), *slash* for cuts (from the blade's impact on: the recording's whoosh is the robot's own swing voice), *cutlass* for the Impala's cutlass (taken whole from its onset: the thump as the blade lands, the tearing through the whole spectrum over a heavy low body, the high lines ringing out past a second; a 1.3 s take). A blow may name its sound (`blowSound`), or `none` when its effect sounds it itself. `node tools/hit-model.mjs <kind>=<file>` refits only the kinds given. `tools/hit-model.mjs` fits each recording as a spectral-envelope model (level over time in 28 third-octave bands) plus its ringing partials (tonal lines that outlast the impact, with their own envelopes), then refines the model until its synthesis measures like the recording (the band filters overlap; the raw fit came out hot where the spectrum is dense). A take is band-passed noise per band shaped by its envelope, plus a sine per partial; four takes a kind differ in noise, a small band tilt, a few per cent of time stretch and a hair of detune. `--compare` prints a take's band-envelope error against the recordings (~2-3 dB by band). The first version hand-built layers (a sine thump gliding down in pitch, a snap, resonator banks) and was rejected as cartoonish: the recorded punch has no pitch sweep (a broadband slap, then a steady ~50 Hz boom swelling in), the stomp's boom comes ~140 ms after its scuff, and the slash's ring is a handful of partials over bright noise. This is the one percussive hit sound, asked for by the user; the soldier's own knock (`impact`) stays under it.

## Tools and tests

- `node tools/enemy-sample.mjs <out> <samples>` renders soldiers, key poses, reactions and citadel views headlessly (`tools/preview/enemies.ts`; `citadel-d<k>` looks out over district k from its yard).
- `BRAWL=<district> node tools/fight-sheet.mjs ...` fights that district's garrison in its yard. The log prints living and destroyed counts; `HITS=1` prints every hit event with the soldiers around it. `G<t0>-<t1>` in the click list holds the guard.
- `tests/enemies.test.ts` seeds `Math.random`, because the AI and debris draw on it. It walks the truck robot from outside the main gate into every district and a soldier between every pair with `CitadelNav`, and checks stand-down, flinch, health and the special's hold. `citadel.test.ts` also checks spawn/beat floor coverage and the same-frame floor and first-blow cases.
