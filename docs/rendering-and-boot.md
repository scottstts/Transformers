# Rendering and startup

The app uses Three's WebGPU backend and TSL node materials. The renderer's built-in WebGL fallback is disabled immediately after construction, and initialization verifies that a WebGPU device was obtained. Device loss and uncaptured GPU errors retain Three's default logging and also stop the game through the app's error path.

Startup remains observed through dynamic module loading, renderer initialization, scene creation, shader compilation, first render, and GPU queue completion. The selected vehicle loader resolves only after both the vehicle and its combat weapon have downloaded and decoded. During shader compilation the hidden combat weapon/effects (and the specials' billows, arcs and the world's debris) are forced through one real draw, including the weapon's cast-shadow path, and the GPU queue is drained before the normal first frame can be declared ready. The entry screen (`index.html`, `platform/boot-ui.ts`) shows boot progress as a count of the seven reported stages (`BOOT_STEPS`); a sun rises over a drawn ridge line and a ruler gauge fills. When the first frame is on the GPU, the gauge opens into the Enter button and the drawing fades to show the live scene behind. The live scene opens on a low broadside of the car from its sunlit side (`FollowCamera.showSide`). The entry type sits in the sky above the car, and the controls legend sits at the bottom edge, so the car stays clear between them. The broadside is only the starting orbit and stays in place after Enter. The first look drag orbits away from it. Once the car moves, the usual drive follow swings the camera in behind. On narrow screens the broadside backs off until the whole car fits across (`SIDE_HALF_SPAN`). That override ends at the first look or movement, and the distance then eases back to the normal framing. On desktop, Enter requests pointer lock, and play starts on `pointerlockchange`, so a refused lock leaves the button there for another try. On touch screens, Enter starts play directly. On failure the same screen becomes the diagnostic view (`#boot-error`). Adding or removing a `setBootStage` call means updating `BOOT_STEPS`.

`#app` is `position: fixed; inset: 0`: the canvas always covers the viewport, and the page background never shows beneath it, even if the document scrolls or resizes oddly.

Resizes commit once per animation frame through `setDrawingBufferSize`. The base DPR is capped at 1.7 and at 4,000,000 drawing-buffer pixels, including when the resulting DPR is below 1. The scene uses ACES tone mapping and a node bloom pass. The post pass reads the scene through the lens reactions of a special (`rendering/lens.ts`: a blast wave's refraction ring, an exposure flash before tone mapping, a drained grade; specials.md); at rest they are exactly neutral. The film grade (`rendering/grade.ts`) runs after tone mapping and output encoding, so it works on display values. The pipeline therefore turns off `outputColorTransform` and calls `renderOutput` itself before the grade. The grade applies a blend toward a smoothstep S-curve for contrast, cool shadows and warm highlights, an elliptical vignette and per-frame hashed grain that is strongest in the mid-tones. It uses arithmetic only, with no extra passes or texture reads. The grain also hides banding in the sky gradient.
